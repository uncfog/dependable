{% load static %} {% comment %} The school's page, not the platform's. This deliberately does NOT extend base.html. That template puts the product name in the , the product mark in the header and the product's favicon in the tab — correct for every screen a member of staff sees, and wrong for every screen a parent sees. A mother following a link from a school's WhatsApp status has come to enquire about that school; introducing her to the software vendor instead answers a question she did not ask, and on a first visit reads like the wrong website. So: the school's name is the title, the school's logo is the mark, and SCHOOLCORD appears nowhere on the page. The one thing shared with the rest of the platform is the stylesheet, because the design system is what makes the form legible on a three-year-old Android phone and none of it is branded.
